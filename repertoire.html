How a
programme
is built

Most clients arrive with two or three pieces they already care about and a vague sense of the rest. That is a good place to start. I ask what the room looks like, how many people are in it, and what happens directly before and after the music. A stone church at four in the afternoon wants a different sound to a rooftop bar at ten at night.

From there I draft a running order with timings, send it over, and we cut or swap until it fits. Ceremony music is timed to the length of the aisle and the number of guests seated. Drinks-hour sets run in twenty to thirty minute blocks with breaks, so the volume never fights conversation.

Custom requests are welcome and included in the fee when the song has a workable melody. Give me three weeks and I will write the arrangement, record a phone-quality preview so you can hear it before the day, and revise it once if something is not sitting right. Songs built entirely on rhythm or vocal effects rarely translate to one violin, and I will say so early rather than let it disappoint you on the day.

By mood and moment

Five sections

I

Processionals & ceremony

The aisle, the register signing, the walk back out. Timed to the room so nothing has to be cut short or padded.

  • Canon in D, Pachelbel
  • Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring
  • Air on the G String, Bach
  • Ave Maria, Schubert
  • Arrival of the Queen of Sheba
  • Wedding March, Mendelssohn

II

Classical standards

Drawn from the solo violin literature and the salon repertoire. Suited to seated dinners, awards and quieter gallery receptions.

  • Meditation from Thaïs, Massenet
  • Winter, Vivaldi (Four Seasons)
  • Partita No. 3 Preludio, Bach
  • Salut d'Amour, Elgar
  • Méditation, Gluck arr. Kreisler
  • Csárdás, Monti

III

Film & screen themes

The most requested category by some distance. Familiar melodies that people recognise within four bars.

  • Schindler's List, Williams
  • Merry-Go-Round of Life, Hisaishi
  • Cinema Paradiso, Morricone
  • Comptine d'un autre été, Amélie
  • Concerning Hobbits, Shore
  • The Godfather, Rota

IV

Jazz & late-night standards

Hotel lounge material, played with a swung feel. Works well from about nine in the evening, with or without a backing track.

  • Fly Me to the Moon
  • Autumn Leaves
  • Blue Moon
  • Cheek to Cheek
  • Minor Swing, Reinhardt
  • La Vie en Rose

V

Pop arrangements

Written out for solo violin rather than played over a karaoke track. Good for first dances and the walk back down the aisle.

  • Can't Help Falling in Love
  • A Thousand Years
  • Perfect, Ed Sheeran
  • At Last, Etta James
  • Yellow, Coldplay
  • Make You Feel My Love
